Every property inspector, surveyor, site engineer or real-estate agent knows the scene: 80 photos in the camera roll, mixed in with family pictures, a Word file open, WhatsApp full of loose images — and a whole evening of work to turn all that into a report someone will take seriously. ReportGoApp was born to eliminate that evening.

I'm a Flutter developer and this is the most ambitious project I've built so far. In this article I show what the app does, how it works under the hood and — for those who follow the vertical-software market — why I believe there is a real opportunity here.

The problem: a good report depends on proof, and proof gets lost along the way

A photo report is only worth something when every photo carries when, where and by whom it was taken. In practice, the field workflow is usually improvised:

How it usually goesWith ReportGoApp
Loose photos in the camera roll, mixed with personal onesPhotos inside the right report, already numbered (Rel.0001-26)
Date, time and place typed by hand — or forgottenGPS and timestamp stored in EXIF and printed on the photo itself
Text in Word, photos pasted one by oneRich-text comment per report and caption per photo
Paper signature, scanned laterHandwritten signature on screen; the report locks once signed
PDF assembled on the computer at nightPDF generated on the phone itself, ready to share
No signal, no workEverything works offline

How it works: from tap to PDF in 5 steps

The app's tagline sums up the idea: "Register. We do the rest."

  1. Create the reportTitle, date and summary. The app assigns the year's sequential number itself (Rel.0001-26), with no risk of duplicates.
  2. Shoot or importCamera or gallery. The app reads the EXIF, applies a watermark with date, time and coordinates, and compresses the image — with no extra work from you.
  3. DescribeRich-text editor for the report and for each photo: bold, lists, highlights. What the field saw, written in the field.
  4. SignThe author signs with a finger on the screen. After that the report is protected against changes — exactly what a formal report needs.
  5. ExportPDF generated on the device, with photos, captions, signature and numbering, ready to send to the client.

Features that make the difference

Photos with evidentiary value

EXIF with GPS and date/time preserved in the final file, plus a visual watermark. Metadata that survives compression.

✍️

Signature and locking

Handwritten signature as PNG. A signed report accepts no new photos or edits — the rule lives in the database, not just on the screen.

🗺️

See where each inspection took place, with filters for places, completed, pending and recent. No Google Maps API costs.

Local data, PBKDF2 password hashing, permissions requested only at the moment of use, and isolation between users on the same device.

Who it's for

For anyone who produces photo-based reports as part of their job: property inspectors, surveyors, engineers and site supervisors, real-estate agents, maintenance technicians and inspectors. In all of these routines the photo must be organized, dated, located and often signed — and the professional rarely has an internet signal where they work.

Under the hood: engineering built to last

For developers (and anyone doing technical due diligence): ReportGoApp is not a prototype. It's an app with a layered architecture and business rules protected by tests.

  • Clean Architecture + MVVM per feature (domain, data, presentation), with Riverpod 3 for dependency injection and state, Freezed for immutable models and Result/Failure for typed errors.
  • Drift (SQLite) with reactive streams. The Rel.0001-26 numbering is atomic: reading the highest number and inserting happen in the same transaction, with a unique key as a second line of defense.
  • Rules in the database: SQLite triggers prevent changing an already-signed report, even if a UI bug tries to. Every query filters by the session user.
  • Photo pipeline with each step isolated in a testable service and heavy image processing kept off the UI thread:
Camera / Gallery
  → permission (only at the moment of use)
  → EXIF read (GPS + date/time)
  → watermark (in an Isolate, off the UI thread)
  → compression
  → EXIF re-injection into the final file
  → private storage + SQLite record
  • Security: PBKDF2-HMAC-SHA256 password hashing with 100k iterations and a random salt, session kept in secure storage, audio permissions removed from the Android manifest.
  • Quality: over 600 automated tests, clean static analysis and validation on two real Samsung devices (not just an emulator).
  • Cloud-ready: UUID primary keys, a sync-state field and repositories decoupled from the database. When the backend arrives, it plugs in as a new data source — no app rewrite.

For investors and partners: the thesis

Vertical software for field professionals is a niche where the typical competitor is improvisation (camera + Word + WhatsApp), not another dominant product. It's where a simple, fast and reliable app can enter without fighting giants for attention.

Recurring pain

Every inspection produces a report. People in this line of work write reports every week — usage is naturally frequent.

Offline-first edge

Works where there's little or no signal: job sites, rural areas, basements. That's the weak spot of many cloud-dependent solutions.

Low operating cost

No backend in the MVP and no map-API billing, so the marginal cost of each new user is very small.

Business model

The product premise is a free app, fully functional offline, with cloud storage as a future paid tier. The monetization paths I'm considering (still being validated, none implemented):

  • Paid cloud plan: backup and sync across devices.
  • Report link: share the report to open directly in a browser or the app.
  • Teams and branding: multiple users per company, report templates and the client's logo on the PDF.
Transparency about the current stage

ReportGoApp is in pre-launch (version 0.1.x): the offline MVP is functional and validated on Android devices. There are no paying users, revenue or retention metrics yet, and I'd rather say so than inflate numbers. What exists is a real, well-built product ready to be tested by people who live this problem.

What I'm looking for now: professionals for free pilots, feedback from people who write reports, and conversations with angel investors, accelerators and mentors interested in vertical software.
